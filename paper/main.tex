\begin{abstract}
We assemble an auditable corner-kick dataset from pinned open event data and
evaluate whether team history improves delivery and shot prediction over pooled
competition-season rates. The configured corpus contains 16,565 corners in 1,632
matches. Chronological league holdouts support a delivery advantage for shrinkage;
the primary shot comparison with pooling shows no clear improvement. Outcome-window
and short-distance checks retain that bounded interpretation. Available tournament
360 frames support retrospective static profiles, not movement or tactical-quality
labels. A completed two-reviewer source-consistency audit agrees on all 400 paired
judgments in an enriched 100-corner sample; it is not independent football ground
truth. Supplied systems evidence documents Python/SQL parity and bounded desktop
timings. A strictly historical dossier demonstrates cutoff enforcement for two teams.
Additional dossier examples, reader evaluation and several scope decisions remain
pending; neither usefulness nor generalization beyond this corpus is established.
\end{abstract}

predicts later delivery choices and shot creation, what shared static setups

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Event data and reproducibility.}
Pappalardo et al.~\cite{pappalardo2019} document an open soccer event collection
with action times, positions, outcomes and player attributes. Their separation
of event logs from tracking data is relevant here: an event stream can support
action histories without reconstructing all players' movement. Our inputs are
StatsBomb records, not their dataset, and provider definitions are not assumed
interchangeable. We retain pinned source identity, event IDs and explicit
sequence rules so that a corner summary can be traced to its recorded evidence.

\paragraph{Spatial corner models.}
TacticAI~\cite{wang2024} uses player graphs built from tracking-derived features
for receiver and shot prediction, retrieval and proposed positional adjustments.
It also includes expert assessment of utility. Our static zone-count clusters
provide a different, descriptive view of partially visible snapshots; they do
not reconstruct those graph inputs or reproduce that system. Team-history
frequency forecasts likewise answer a different question from predicting a
receiver from a particular spatial setup. We make no direct performance
comparison across these different tasks, data and evaluation designs.

\paragraph{Probability evaluation.}
Gneiting and Raftery~\cite{gneiting2007} develop proper scoring rules, including
logarithmic and quadratic scores, for evaluating probabilistic forecasts. This
supports scoring the full delivery distribution and shot probability rather
than only their most likely labels. It does not validate our label construction
or any particular smoothing value. In this study the fixed validation grid
selects smoothing before test scoring. The raw delivery baseline's floor changes
its scored distribution, and label-threshold changes alter the comparison
population; both qualifications remain necessary when interpreting losses.

\paragraph{Prediction and use.}
Davis et al.~\cite{davis2024} discuss dependence-aware evaluation and distinguish
an indicator's utility from the accuracy of its underlying model. We therefore
separate chronological prediction, retrospective spatial description and the
cutoff-safe report demonstration. Our match bootstrap addresses within-match
dependence conditionally on fitted models, not every source of uncertainty.
Held-out score improvements and a reproducible dossier do not establish reader
usefulness. The pending reader evaluation and additional team examples are
separate evidence requirements, rather than conclusions inferred from prediction.

\section{Ingestion and corner extraction}
\subsection{Input identity and schema}
The ingestion pipeline caches provider JSON and records status and SHA-256
receipts for each file. The batch manifest links match IDs to event paths, source
revision, competition, season and date. Conflicting duplicate IDs reject a match; exact
duplicates are removed from the cleaned ingestion representation. Raw inputs remain
unchanged. Missing optional attributes remain missing, and optional spatial-file
failure is distinguished from valid event ingestion. Valid zero-corner matches
remain manifest entries. The checked primary tables have no duplicate match/corner
keys or missing required identifiers and competition/season/date metadata.

One summary row represents one corner, retaining its source ID, taker, period,
timestamp, possession, delivery attributes and coordinates, applied settings,
attacking shot and goal counts, provider xG sum, ordered shot/chain IDs and stop
reason. CSV and typed Parquet are exported separately from raw data. Provider xG
is a descriptive provider quantity, not an independently fitted model here.
This working provider-format contract does not by itself establish the proposed
kloppy/lab canonical-schema integration or every initially proposed export field.

\subsection{Starters, ordering and outcome boundaries}
The runner orders events by period, timestamp and provider index, preserving source
order for ties. A starter is a Pass or Shot whose nested type is Corner; the broader
From Corner flag does not create a new kick. The first qualifying event per ID is
retained. A direct corner shot is included as its own attacking attempt.

Starting with the kick, each subsequent unique event is checked in this priority:
period change; elapsed time strictly greater than the cap; new restart; possession
change; explicit stoppage or non-advantage foul. Stoppages include Half End, Injury Stoppage,
Offside and Referee Ball-Drop. These pre-inclusion boundary events
are excluded. Otherwise the action is included, an attacking Shot is counted,
and a terminal goal or top-level out flag closes the sequence after inclusion.
Events exactly at the cap remain eligible. Primary extraction uses 15 seconds;
uncapped removes only time and retains the other rules. Pressure alone does not
change possession, and a foul with advantage does not itself stop play. Half End
is a stoppage if no earlier-priority boundary already applies.

These are recorded-event rules, not measurements of physical live-play duration.
An off-target shot may lack an explicit out flag. A later time-cap boundary does
not establish continuous play. A nested pass outcome Out without a
top-level out flag is not a boundary under the current specification. Shot events,
goals, and corners producing at least one shot are different counts. Changing a
chain definition changes attribution, not the underlying match or a causal effect.

\subsection{Delivery definitions}
Direct corner shots form their own category. A pass is short if it is a Ground
Pass with finite start/end coordinates and Euclidean distance no greater than
15 native provider units. This derived category takes precedence over technique.
Otherwise recognized Inswinging, Outswinging and Straight techniques determine
the category; remaining passes are unknown. Exported technique is preserved
independently. An exported Unknown value alone does not prove the raw provider
field was absent. Unknowns remain in descriptive totals and shot analysis; leaving
them out of delivery scoring is a documented analysis policy, not an extraction
finding. Section~\ref{sec:short} reports the preplanned 10/\allowbreak{}20-unit alternatives.

\section{Source-consistency audit}
Diego and Asaad reviewed the same frozen 100-corner sample, enriched for rare
deliveries and boundary cases. Both sheets preserve the frozen fields of the
hash-authenticated master. All 400 paired judgments agree. Each field has 100\%
pre-discussion agreement: delivery, shot count, goal count and stop reason.
All answers are yes, so Cohen's kappa is undefined because its denominator is
zero; we do not report it as 1.0. Six note-flagged termination cases have separate,
jointly confirmed rule-consistent resolutions dated 25 November 2026. No extractor
error was identified under the audited rules.

This enriched source-consistency audit is neither independent football ground
truth nor an unbiased corpus-wide accuracy estimate. Automated consistency checks
remain separate from human judgments. Post-audit explanations clarify existing
boundary priority; they do not change the original answers or agreement.

Audit source traceability was separately investigated. A restored local match
JSON matches the original recorded sidecar/extraction hash, and the altered backup
is retained. The structural difference is one corner identifier at provider index
2048. This establishes local recorded-byte identity, not a fresh upstream download
or proof of which bytes every historical reviewer accessed. Available computational
receipts identify the original source hash; no affected saved computational run is
identified, while path-only and unlogged historical reads remain unverifiable.
Previously inaccessible combined preparation records now match their recorded
input hashes. The supplied report's broader raw-chain derivation and full-cache
census are not promoted to fresh independent reproductions by this manuscript.

\section{Prediction methods and evaluation}
\subsection{Chronological design and eligibility}
Within each competition-season, matches are sorted by date and then numeric ID.
The first $\lfloor0.6M\rfloor$ matches train, the next through
$\lfloor0.8M\rfloor$ validate, and the remainder test. Every corner of a match
stays together. Missing-date matches are excluded. Team eligibility is fixed using
at least 10 training corner-observed matches and 30 training corners; a corner-only
table cannot recover zero-corner team appearances. Sparse teams remain descriptive.
Tournaments do not meet this training-history rule and have no scored predictions.

Whole matches remain intact, but date/ID tie-breaking does not guarantee that all
same-date league fixtures fall in one partition. The dossier uses a stricter cutoff that excludes the whole target date. Neither eligibility nor
method choices are changed using held-out outcomes.

\subsection{Pooled, raw and shrunk history}
For known delivery category $d$, let $n_{td}$ and $N_t$ be a team's historical
category and total known-delivery counts in competition-season $c$. The five
scored labels are short, inswinging, outswinging, straight and direct shot.
The pooled prior and shrunk estimate are
\begin{equation}
 p_{cd}=\frac{n_{cd}+1}{N_c+5},\qquad
 \widehat p_{td}=\frac{n_{td}+\alpha p_{cd}}{N_t+\alpha}.
\end{equation}
The unsmoothed baseline uses team frequencies, floors probabilities at $10^{-6}$
and renormalizes to avoid infinite log loss. This floor also affects zero-weight
shrinkage. With no known team delivery history the implementation falls back to
the pooled distribution. The comparison with the unsmoothed baseline is therefore
not a floor-independent comparison against every reasonable history model.

Shot probability uses all corners, not only known delivery labels. With $S_c$
shot-producing corners among $C_c$ historical corners, the pooled binary prior is
$(S_c+1)/(C_c+2)$. Unsmoothed team shot frequency is $S_t/C_t$ and shrinkage is
$(S_t+\beta p_c)/(C_t+\beta)$. Goals remain descriptive because they are sparse;
they are not a scored prediction target.

\subsection{Selection, scoring and uncertainty}
Alpha minimizes validation multiclass log loss; beta separately minimizes Brier
score on the binary shot-producing indicator. Both use the fixed grid
$\{0,1,2,5,10,20,50,100,200,500,1000\}$, with ties favouring larger weights.
Final fitting uses training plus validation, preserving training-only eligibility.
Test data are scored without changing the grid or methods. Delivery and shot
availability are handled separately: absent scorable validation delivery labels
do not invent alpha or suppress a valid shot evaluation. Unavailable values stay
unavailable rather than becoming zero.

Delivery scores omit unknown labels, while shot scores retain those corners.
This can make the scored delivery population selective. The saved 95\% percentile
intervals use 1,000 match-bootstrap draws and seed 5118. Resampling whole matches
keeps both teams' corners together; paired method contrasts use the same sampled
matches. These intervals condition on fitted models and selected weights, rather
than quantifying complete fitting/selection uncertainty or all team dependence.
The reliability CSV is available; no absent reliability figure is invented.

delivery attributes, short threshold, splits, eligibility, methods and bootstrap
controls. Each window selects its parameters using its own validation data, not
test scores. These reuse the same matches and are robustness analyses, not

\section{Retrospective spatial analysis}
\subsection{Coverage, features and shared clustering}
Events join to delivery-time frames by exact match ID and event UUID. Unavailable
or invalid files are distinguished from ordinary unmatched events. Usable frames
require valid geometry, a starter with each coordinate within three units of the appropriate corner flag, at least
five visible players and penalty-box coverage at least 0.5. Missing and excluded
corners remain visible in coverage outputs.

An eligible snapshot becomes an 18-number vector: attacking and defending outfield
occupancy in nine penalty-box zones, excluding keepers and the taker. One pooled,
deterministic k-means fit spans both tournaments at fixed k=4 and seed 0; teams
share cluster IDs rather than being clustered independently. Saved stability is
the mean adjusted Rand index over ten 80\% subsample refits with reassignment.
ARI measures resampling consistency, not tactical meaning or correctness.

Table~\ref{tab:spatial-coverage} separates matching from usable coverage: 592 of
1,083 corners match frames and 591 are eligible; one matched frame fails visibility.
The pooled clusters have 228/\allowbreak{}163/\allowbreak{}115/\allowbreak{}85 members and saved ARI 0.864. There are 56
competition-season-team profiles and 224 team-cluster rows. Shares divide by each
team's eligible corners and sum to one. Team eligible counts range from 1 to 24;
none is actually zero, while zero-eligible handling is supported by implementation
tests and retains undefined shares. This does not make individual profiles precise.

Short deliveries match frames less often than non-short deliveries: 19/\allowbreak{}65 versus
283/\allowbreak{}506 at the World Cup, and 20/\allowbreak{}62 versus 270/\allowbreak{}450 at Euro. These are descriptive,
match-clustered comparisons, not evidence of why frames are absent. Similar rates

\subsection{Zone orientation and figure interpretation}
The verified feature definition attacks toward increasing x on a 120-by-80 pitch,
with the goal line at x=120. The box spans x=102--120 and y=18--62. Its depth edges
are 102,108,114,120; width edges are 18,$18+44/\allowbreak{}3$,$18+88/\allowbreak{}3$,62. Corners near y=0
are mirrored by $y\mapsto80-y$, so the normalized reference flag is the high-y flag
(x=120,y=80).

In the supplied raster, columns move \emph{left to right toward the goal}:
z1/z4/z7 occupy x=102--108, z2/z5/z8 occupy x=108--114, and z3/z6/z9 occupy
x=114--120. Rows increase in y \emph{down the displayed grid}: z1--z3 are the low-y
band, z4--z6 the middle, and z7--z9 the high-y, corner-side band. Thus the top row
of the image is not the normalized corner-side touchline; the high-y corner lies
beyond its bottom edge. These schematic cells are not a pitch-scale image.

\section{Limitations and remaining work}
The selected seasons and provider labels limit generalization. Missing techniques
and out flags, selective scoring of known deliveries and incomplete spatial
visibility constrain interpretation. Match bootstrap intervals do not capture
all team dependence or uncertainty from selection and refitting. Attributed
outcomes and static clusters do not establish delivery effectiveness or tactical
success. The enriched audit checks consistency with source records rather than
independent football truth. Its undefined kappa does not measure reliability
across varied judgment labels.

The completed distance and window checks leave proposal deviations unresolved:
bounded corpus size, unverified equivalence with the kloppy/lab schema, missing
evidence for duration and shot-location exports or target-zone displays, context
analyses, and the exploratory spatial protocol. These need explicit acceptance
or further evidence. The related-work synthesis still needs owner review.
Author identities, affiliations, owner sign-off and final layout approval are
unfinished. The separate unresolved register maps gaps to evidence and owners.

\section{Conclusion}
In this fixed corpus, the chronological evaluation supports better delivery
prediction with shrinkage than with pooling. The planned label and window checks
retain that conclusion. Historical shot frequency shows no clear primary gain
over pooling. Partial 360 data support retrospective static summaries. The
past-only dossier demonstrates source traceability and cutoff handling. These
results support an auditable descriptive workflow without establishing causal
football effects, general tactical routines or a validated scouting product.
Additional dossiers, reader evaluation and accepted scope decisions remain
unfinished; extra text cannot replace that evidence.
